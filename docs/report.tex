\begin{abstract}
Threshold secret sharing distributes a secret so that any $t$ of $n$ shares recover it and any
$t-1$ reveal nothing. Classical schemes guarantee confidentiality but say nothing about integrity:
a shareholder who receives a corrupted share learns only that reconstruction failed, not who
corrupted it. Verifiable variants (Feldman, Pedersen) detect inconsistency at the cost of a
discrete-logarithm group and still cannot identify the culprit. ShareLock keeps the scheme in the
byte-oriented field $\mathrm{GF}(2^8)$, commits to every share under a Merkle root bound to a
session identifier, and thereby (i) verifies any single share in $O(\log n)$ hashes, (ii) names the
exact indices of corrupted shares, and (iii) refuses shares replayed from another session.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem}
A secret $s \in \mathrm{GF}(2^8)^k$ is split among $n$ holders with threshold $t$. Three properties
are wanted:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Confidentiality:} any set of fewer than $t$ shares yields no information about $s$.
  \item \textbf{Reconstructability:} any $t$ shares determine $s$.
  \item \textbf{Accountability:} if some shares are corrupted, the receiver learns \emph{which}.
\end{enumerate}
Shamir's construction gives (1) and (2). Property (3) is what this work adds without leaving the byte field.

\section{Preliminaries}
\paragraph{The field.} $\mathrm{GF}(2^8)$ is realised with the irreducible polynomial
$m(x) = x^8 + x^4 + x^3 + x + 1$ (the AES modulus). Addition is bitwise XOR; multiplication is
carried out with logarithm tables generated by the primitive element $3$. Every non-zero element is
invertible, so Lagrange interpolation is available at any set of distinct points.

\paragraph{Shamir sharing.} For each byte $s_j$ of the secret, the dealer samples
$a_{j,1},\dots,a_{j,t-1} \gets \mathrm{GF}(2^8)$ uniformly and sets
$f_j(x) = s_j + \sum_{i=1}^{t-1} a_{j,i} x^i$. Share $i$ is $\bigl(x_i, (f_1(x_i),\dots,f_k(x_i))\bigr)$
for distinct non-zero $x_i$. Reconstruction uses the Lagrange coefficients
$\lambda_i = \prod_{j \ne i} \frac{x_j}{x_j - x_i}$ and computes $s = \sum_i \lambda_i y_i$.

\section{Construction}
Let $H$ be SHA-256 and $\sigma$ a 128-bit session identifier.

\begin{definition}[Share commitment]
For share $i$ with coordinate $x_i$ and payload $y_i$,
\[
  L_i = H\bigl(\texttt{"sharelock/v1/share"} \,\|\, |\sigma| \,\|\, \sigma \,\|\, i \,\|\, x_i \,\|\, |y_i| \,\|\, y_i\bigr).
\]
The Merkle root $R$ over $(L_1,\dots,L_n)$ with node function
$N(u,v) = H(\texttt{"sharelock/v1/node"} \| u \| v)$ is published together with $\sigma$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Verification]
A share $i$ is \emph{valid} iff recomputing $L_i$ and folding it with its Merkle path reproduces
$R$ under the published $\sigma$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Confidentiality]
Let $S \subset \{1,\dots,n\}$, $|S| \le t-1$, and let $(x_i, y_i)_{i \in S}$ be observed. For every
candidate secret $s' \in \mathrm{GF}(2^8)^k$ there exists a polynomial vector of degree $\le t-1$
consistent with the observations and with $f(0) = s'$. Consequently the posterior distribution of
$s$ given the observations equals its prior.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a byte position. Let $g$ be the unique polynomial of degree $\le |S|-1 \le t-2$ interpolating
the observed points $(x_i, y_i)_{i \in S}$, and let
$h(x) = \prod_{i \in S} (x - x_i)$. Define $f(x) = g(x) + c\,h(x)$ where
$c = \bigl(s' - g(0)\bigr) \cdot h(0)^{-1}$. Because $h(0) \ne 0$ (all $x_i$ are non-zero and
distinct) and the field is a field, $c$ is well defined. Then for every $i \in S$,
$h(x_i) = 0$ so $f(x_i) = g(x_i) = y_i$, and $f(0) = g(0) + c\,h(0) = s'$. The degree of $f$ is at
most $t-1$. Repeating independently for each of the $k$ bytes gives a consistent sharing of $s'$.
Since the dealer's coefficients are uniform and independent, every $s'$ arises with equal
probability, so the observations carry zero information about $s$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Integrity and localisation]
Assume $H$ is collision resistant. If share $i$ is modified after commitment, verification of share
$i$ fails; verification of every unmodified share $j \ne i$ succeeds.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A modification to $y_i$ or $x_i$ changes the preimage of $L_i$; collision resistance of $H$ implies
the recomputed leaf differs from the committed one, and the Merkle fold is a deterministic function
of the leaf and its path, so it cannot reproduce $R$ unless a collision is found. For $j \ne i$ the
leaf $L_j$ is untouched and its path consists of sibling hashes none of which is $L_i$ (the path
covers the complement of $j$'s subtree), so the fold still yields $R$. Hence the failing index set
is exactly $\{i\}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Session binding]
A share committed under $\sigma$ fails verification under any $\sigma' \ne \sigma$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$\sigma$ is part of every leaf preimage and is compared against the commitment before any hashing
(the comparison is constant time). For $\sigma' \ne \sigma$ the recomputed leaf differs from the
committed leaf, and the fold cannot reach $R$ without a hash collision.
\end{proof}

\section{Attack surface}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Dealer trust.} A malicious dealer can commit to a wrong sharing and still produce a
  valid root. ShareLock detects transport and storage corruption, not a lying dealer; a
  commitment to the secret itself would be needed for that and is out of scope.
  \item \textbf{Root authenticity.} $R$ must reach verifiers over an authenticated channel;
  otherwise an adversary substitutes a root for shares of its own choosing.
  \item \textbf{Confidentiality of shares.} A hash commits without hiding. Shares still require a
  confidential channel; this scheme does not replace one.
  \item \textbf{Timing.} Session comparison uses \texttt{hmac.compare_digest}; the Merkle fold is
  data independent, so verification time does not depend on how close a forgery came.
  \item \textbf{Share reuse.} Shares are bound to $(\sigma, i)$; replaying a share from another
  session fails the binding check.
\end{itemize}

\section{Comparison}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
Scheme & Field & Verifiable & Localises & Group assumption \\
\midrule
Shamir \cite{shamir} & any field & no & no & none \\
Feldman VSS \cite{feldman} & $\mathbb{Z}_p$ & yes & no & discrete log \\
Pedersen VSS \cite{pedersen} & $\mathbb{Z}_p$ & yes & no & discrete log \\
ShareLock & $\mathrm{GF}(2^8)$ & yes & \textbf{yes} & none (hash only) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Feldman and Pedersen check a linear relation over a group; a single bad share breaks the relation for
the whole set without revealing which share caused it. ShareLock commits per share, so the failing
index is recovered in $O(\log n)$ hashes per shareholder and no group is required, which matters on
constrained devices where byte-field arithmetic is already available (e.g.\ AES hardware).

\section{Implementation and measurements}
Reference implementation in Python 3.13 (\texttt{sharelock/}), 14 tests, no third-party
dependencies. Secret length 32 bytes, 20 repetitions:

\toprule
$(t,n)$ & share bytes & expansion & split p50 & combine p50 & locate p50 \\
\midrule
$(2,3)$ & 99 & $3.09\times$ & 0.012 ms & 0.008 ms & 0.009 ms \\
$(3,5)$ & 165 & $5.16\times$ & 0.037 ms & 0.013 ms & 0.021 ms \\

Verification of one share costs 0.003--0.004 ms and a proof of $\log_2 n \cdot 32$ bytes
(64 bytes at $n=3$, 96 at $n=5$). Locating all corrupted shares is a full pass over the set.

\section{Limitations}
Storage overhead is the obvious cost: shares total $n \cdot k$ bytes plus one coordinate byte each,
which is inherent to information-theoretic sharing and cannot be reduced without relaxing the
threshold. The scheme does not protect against a malicious dealer, does not hide shares, and assumes
an authenticated channel for the Merkle root. Constant-time behaviour is argued for comparison and
the fold, but the Python implementation is not a side-channel-hardened artefact.
